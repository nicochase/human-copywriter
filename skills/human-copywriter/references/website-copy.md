# Commercial website copy

Start with the reader's actual problem, the offer and the next action. Write an argument appropriate to that page, rather than filling a universal template.

## Drafting decisions

- Explain what the product/service is and who benefits before relying on cleverness.
- Connect a real capability to a reader consequence. Quantify only when evidence supports the number; specificity can come from a mechanism, example or constraint instead.
- Identify a meaningful differentiator. If none is supported, ask or flag the gap; don't manufacture superiority.
- Arrange sections in the order readers need to understand and decide. Proof, how it works, objections and next steps belong where they resolve actual uncertainty.
- Use the language customers actually use without importing unsupported claims from their wording.
- Each section should advance the argument. A section can contain several related facts; the rule is coherence, not a sentence quota.

Homepage: make the offer clear and help relevant audiences choose a path. Landing page: keep the promise continuous with the traffic source and focus on the intended action. Service/product page: explain scope, use cases, evidence and constraints. Pricing page: make actual plans, billing conditions and differences understandable. About page: tell the true story and connect it to the audience's reason to trust or care.

## Persuasion and proof

Use concrete benefits and relevant proof without exaggerating pain. Real objections are useful; invented strawmen are not. Emotional intensity should fit the brand and stakes. Informative writing can persuade without fear or inflated urgency.

Explain existing offer terms accurately: deliverable, timing, customer effort, price/payment conditions, support, guarantees and cancellation. Missing terms are gaps to flag, not business decisions to make. Don't add bonuses, a refund promise, a deadline or scarce capacity merely because a framework recommends them. Use actual availability or expiry only with a reliable basis.

Treat positioning and conversion ideas as hypotheses. A formula or an editorial score cannot establish a conversion lift. Do not import the upstream repos' unsourced numerical lift claims.

## Headline and CTA

A strong headline makes a relevant, supportable promise or plainly names the offer. Formulas such as category + audience or outcome + mechanism are starting points; avoid formula-shaped language if it sounds generic. Never promise “never again” when the outcome isn't guaranteed.

A CTA tells readers what happens next. “See pricing” or “Contact us” may be exactly right; clarity is more useful than a universal blacklist. Microcopy may explain actual trial, payment or privacy conditions. Keep “no card required” only if true. Avoid inventing reassurance.

When alternatives help, vary the argument: clear category, specific mechanism, or supported outcome. Don't produce a quota of near-duplicates. Copy location and above-the-fold repetition depend on the layout and reader task.

Preserve useful headings, lists and tables for scanning. Maintain accurate form labels and error/confirmation messages when in scope; don't hide information essential to completing an action. Finish with the shared substantive editing and Humanizer passes.
