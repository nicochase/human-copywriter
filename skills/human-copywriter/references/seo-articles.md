# SEO articles and content refresh

An article should help the reader complete the search task. SEO supports useful writing; it does not justify padding, copied outlines or arbitrary word counts.

## Brief and research

Use the supplied keyword/intent brief. If the task calls for research and tools are available, inspect current results and relevant primary sources. Record the actual query, locale if important, date and pages examined. Separate observed result patterns from hypotheses; don't claim a ranking cause merely because a format appears often.

Identify the reader's question, expected answer type, required coverage, useful follow-up questions, available original evidence and a specific contribution beyond summarizing competitors. If requested research is unavailable, draft from the provided brief when possible and disclose that material limit. When the user explicitly requests brief-only writing, no routine “no research conducted” note is needed. Do not invent keyword volumes, difficulty scores, People Also Ask results or performed product tests.

Reader value can come from a clearer explanation, a genuine example, a decision framework, an actual comparison or provided firsthand evidence. Do not invent author expertise, customer stories or “I tested this” statements to create that value.

## Draft

Answer direct informational questions early when that fits the format, then explain. A narrative or commercial intent may need a different opening. Match the requested scope and audience; do not force a sales hero/problem/agitation/CTA structure onto an explanation.

Use descriptive H1/H2/H3 headings and natural terminology. Retain exact required keywords where the brief requires them, but don't force repetitions, exact-match anchors or keyword density. Preserve technical terms when they are the correct words. Explain unfamiliar concepts when the audience needs it.

A brief's list of topics says what to cover, not the outline or how much room each topic gets. Lead with the part that matters most to the reader's task, merge related minor points, and let a simple point take one sentence inside another section. Equal headings for every brief item read like a checklist.

Give each section the space its substance needs. Do not require the same lesson, example, takeaway sequence under every heading. A simple instruction can be short; expand difficult decisions, relevant exceptions and supported explanations. Preserve scannability rather than forcing artificial unevenness. A recap is optional once the reader has the answer.

Use tables, steps or examples when they make an answer easier to apply. Lists and FAQs are optional, not mandatory “AI citation” blocks. Supported next-step links or modest relevant product mentions can be useful; avoid making every paragraph an advertisement.

Add citations near claims when requested or necessary for trust. Link to evidence actually inspected; supplied sources can be cited as supplied, not independently verified. Illustrations must read plainly as examples; placeholder names and everyday scenes are fine, a story presented as real is not. Prefer actual supplied cases when available. Distinguish illustration from real findings. Keep estimates, dates and uncertainty visible where meaning depends on them.

For a full article, provide a truthful SEO title and meta description if useful. They should match the content and avoid inflated promises. Display-length ranges are practical heuristics, not ranking requirements. Provide an existing/recommended slug only when requested or needed; don't change a live URL casually. Suggest only relevant internal destinations that exist, or label a proposed destination clearly.

## Refresh existing content

Preserve the live URL, meaningful anchors, supported claims, strong passages and existing conversion paths unless the user authorizes a change or correction is required. Prioritize stale facts, broken resources, missing answers and changes in the actual product. Rewriting everything isn't automatically better.

Do not update a year/date just to imply freshness. Substantial new information should justify a freshness claim. If rankings overlap, diagnose intent and performance before recommending a merge or redirect; shared keywords alone don't prove duplication.

Humanizer should improve the article's voice while retaining the answer, substantive coverage, headings, citations, links and appropriate standalone context. The final pass checks these invariants; it is not another opportunity to add unverified facts.

## Primary guidance

- [Google: helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content): original value, accuracy and no preferred word count.
- [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features): ordinary SEO fundamentals apply; no special AI text files or schema are required.

Check current primary documentation before making a platform-specific recommendation. This writing skill does not implement technical SEO, schema or site migrations.
