# Inspectable writing evaluation

Use when testing the skill or when the user requests an editorial audit, not as mandatory output for ordinary writing.

Retain four observable artifacts, in the user's requested format or an authorized test workspace:

1. Initial draft from the supplied brief.
2. Short editorial findings quoting the specific passages and explaining their problems. Inspect usefulness, whole-piece rhetorical repetition, instructional rhythm (opening streaks, conditional pileup, same-shape sections, even coverage), evidence status of examples, voice, claim strength and any em or en dashes. Do not force a fixed count or invent defects in good prose.
3. Revised final after substantive editing and the Humanizer pass.
4. Factual comparison against the brief and initial draft: added/removed claims, numbers and verbal quantities, named entities, conditions, speaker, advice versus promise, quotes, links, headings and CTA intent. Note deliberate factual corrections separately. Repair any failed invariant and recheck the affected passage.

For already-good input, the draft and final may be identical. Preserve the evidence of that decision. Do not pad a requested short edit into an article.

For version comparisons, freeze realistic briefs before generation and keep the model/settings and inputs equivalent. Include fresh subjects, commercial terms, sparse evidence, authentic voice samples and clean prose. Separate evidence failures from subjective style preferences. Compare outputs without version labels when practical. Treat detector feedback as a secondary, context-dependent signal, not proof of authorship or a quality score.

A separate reviewer call can be useful when available and authorized, but this skill file does not enforce independent execution. A critique within one session must be described as such. Do not claim a blind review, separate agent, performed research or measurable improvement without having conducted it. Keep private brand material out of the reusable package and public test reports.
